Hap

Privacy policy

What personal information Hap handles, why, and your rights under South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA).

Updated 4 November 2026

In short

Your diary is stored on your phone. Hap works without an account, and then no one but you can see your diary.

If you sign in, Hap backs your diary up so you can restore it or use it on another phone. A food diary and weight are health information, so Hap asks for your consent first.

There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your information is never sold or shared for marketing.

Who is responsible

Hap is made by Aubrey Kurten, an individual developer in South Africa, who is the responsible party for your personal information under POPIA and Hap's information officer.

Email: hapapp.za@gmail.com. Postal address: available on request from hapapp.za@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log is stored on your phone: foods, amounts, meals, dates and times, water, weight, targets, the foods you create, your saved meals and your reminder settings.

If you ask Hap to estimate a target, the sex, age, height and weight you enter are used on the phone for that estimate. Only the weight is kept, and only if you choose to log it.

Without an account, none of this leaves your phone and Hap's developer has no access to it. Deleting the app deletes it.

What Hap holds when you back up

Signing in is optional. If you do, Hap processes your email address and an account ID, to sign you in; your diary as listed above, except reminder settings, which stay on the phone, to back it up and keep your phones in step; and technical information such as IP addresses, sign-in times and the app's requests, which the server records for a short time to keep the service running and secure.

Hap processes your email address and account ID to provide the backup you asked for (POPIA section 11(1)(b)). Your diary includes health information, which POPIA treats as special personal information, so Hap backs it up only with your express consent (sections 11(1)(a) and 27(1)(a)). You give it when you sign in, and you can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account.

Searching and scanning

What you search for and the barcodes you scan are sent to Hap's food catalog and to Open Food Facts to find matching foods. They aren't linked to your account. Hap keeps no history of your searches, though the server's short-lived logs may record barcodes looked up.

Open Food Facts is an independent non-profit in France. It receives these requests directly, can see your IP address, and its own privacy policy applies to them.

Camera, reminders and widgets

The camera is used only while the scanner is open, to read barcodes. No photos or video are saved or sent.

Reminders are scheduled on your phone. The Today widget and the Live Activity read your diary on the phone.

Who else handles your information

Supabase Inc. hosts Hap's backup, sign-in and food catalog, under a contract that requires it to protect your information and use it only to provide its service to Hap.

Google (Gmail) delivers the sign-in emails, so it handles your email address and those messages.

Apple distributes Hap through the App Store, under Apple's own privacy policy.

Hap shares your information with no one else, unless the law requires it, for example under a court order.

Where it is stored

The backup is stored in Ireland, in the European Union. EU law (the GDPR) protects personal information at least as well as POPIA, as POPIA section 72 requires for a transfer abroad. Your consent when you sign in also covers this transfer.

How long it is kept

On your phone: until you delete it, or the app.

In the backup: until you delete your account. That removes your account and backup from the server straight away; copies in the hosting provider's routine backups are deleted automatically as those backups expire.

Technical logs: a few days.

Keeping it safe

Your information travels encrypted (HTTPS), and the backup is encrypted where it is stored. The server lets each account read and change only its own information. Hap's developer reaches the database only to run and repair the service.

If a security breach affects your information, Hap will tell you and the Information Regulator as soon as reasonably possible, as POPIA section 22 requires.

Your rights

Under POPIA you can ask what personal information Hap holds about you, have it corrected or deleted, object to how it is processed, and withdraw your consent.

You can do most of this in the app: change or delete any entry, export your diary in Settings, Export, and delete your account in Settings, Account. For anything else, email hapapp.za@gmail.com. Hap replies within 30 days, and may ask you to confirm that the request comes from your account.

If you're unhappy with how Hap handles your information, please tell us first so we can try to put it right. You can also complain to the Information Regulator: POPIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za, 010 023 5200, inforegulator.org.za.

Children

Hap is meant for adults. Anyone under 18 may use it only with the consent of a parent or guardian, who must agree before a backup account is created. If Hap learns that it holds a child's information without that consent, it deletes it.

What Hap doesn't do

No advertising, analytics or tracking tools, and no profiles for marketing. No decisions about you are made automatically. Hap doesn't read or write Apple Health, and it emails you only to sign you in, or about important changes to this policy or your account.

Changes

When this policy changes, the new version is published with a new date. If a change affects how your information is used, Hap tells account holders by email before it takes effect, and asks for consent again where the law requires it.
