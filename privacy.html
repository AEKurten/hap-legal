Hap

Privacy policy

What personal information Hap handles, why, and your rights under South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA).

Updated 12 November 2026

In short

Your diary is stored on your phone. Hap works without an account, and then no one but you can see your diary.

If you sign in, Hap backs your diary up so you can restore it or use it on another phone. A food diary and weight are health information, so Hap asks for your consent first.

If you share a food you created, other Hap users can find it once it has been checked, but they don't see who shared it.

If you log a meal or read a recipe from a photo, the photo is sent to Claude, an AI from Anthropic, to suggest what's in it. Hap doesn't keep it.

If you connect Apple Health or Health Connect, Hap reads your workouts and weigh-ins on your phone, for rewards and Progress. They never leave your phone.

There are no ads and no tracking, and Hap has no analytics of its own. Your information is never sold or shared for marketing.

Hap is made by Aubrey Kurten, an individual developer in South Africa, who is the responsible party for your personal information under POPIA and Hap's information officer. On Google Play, Hap is published under the developer name ChenexaSoftware.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log is stored on your phone: foods, amounts, meals, dates and times, water, weight, targets, the foods you create, your saved meals, your recipes and your reminder settings.

If you ask Hap to estimate a target, the sex, age, height and weight you enter are used on the phone for that estimate. Only the weight is kept, and only if you choose to log it.

Without an account, none of this leaves your phone and Hap's developer has no access to it. Deleting the app deletes it.

What Hap holds when you back up

Signing in is optional. If you do, Hap processes your email address and an account ID, to sign you in; your diary as listed above, except reminder settings, which stay on the phone, to back it up and keep your phones in step; and technical information such as IP addresses, sign-in times and the app's requests, which the server records for a short time to keep the service running and secure.

Hap processes your email address and account ID to provide the backup you asked for (POPIA section 11(1)(b)). Your diary includes health information, which POPIA treats as special personal information, so Hap backs it up only with your express consent (sections 11(1)(a) and 27(1)(a)). You give it when you sign in, and you can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account.

Searching and scanning

What you search for and the barcodes you scan are sent to Hap's food catalog and to Open Food Facts to find matching foods. They aren't linked to your account. Hap keeps no history of your searches, though the server's short-lived logs may record barcodes looked up.

Open Food Facts is an independent non-profit in France. It receives these requests directly, can see your IP address, and its own privacy policy applies to them.

Foods you share

If you're signed in and choose to share a food you created after a scan found nothing, or values you filled in or corrected for a product, Hap stores its barcode, name, brand, serving and nutrition values on its server. Hap's developer checks each one before anyone else can find it. After that, anyone who scans that barcode in Hap sees it. Your name and email aren't shown with it.

Hap shares a food only when you choose to. It keeps your account ID with each food you share, to stop an account that misuses sharing (POPIA section 11(1)(f)). Please don't put personal information in a food you share.

If you report a food, Hap stores the reason, the food as Hap showed it, and your account ID if you're signed in, so that each account counts once.

Deleting your account removes your account ID from the foods you shared and the reports you made. The foods themselves stay, because other people may rely on them.

Meal and recipe photos

If you're signed in and choose to log a meal from a photo, Hap sends that photo through its server to Anthropic, which runs the Claude AI, to suggest the foods in it and how much. The suggestion comes back to your phone for you to check and change. Neither Hap nor its server keeps the photo or the suggestion; only what you log is stored, like any other entry.

If you choose to read a recipe from a photo, Hap sends it the same way, to read the recipe's name, ingredients, amounts and method. They come back for you to check before saving, and the recipe you save is stored like any other.

A photo can show more than food, such as your hands, other people or your home. Please photograph only the food or the recipe.

Hap counts how many photos each account sends in a day, to keep the cost of the service down, and keeps that count for two days.

You choose each photo that is sent, after Hap tells you where it goes; that is your consent (POPIA section 11(1)(a)).

Apple Health and Health Connect

Connecting to Apple Health on an iPhone, or Health Connect on Android, is optional. Health Connect is where Samsung Health and other apps share their data on Android. If you connect, from Rewards or Settings, and allow it on the screen your phone shows, Hap reads the workouts (type, start time and length, and on an iPhone active energy) and the weights saved there, for example by a watch, a gym app or smart scales.

Hap uses them only on your phone: workouts earn XP and badges, and weights you didn't log in Hap show in Progress. Hap doesn't copy them into your diary, back them up, send them to its server or anyone else, or use them for advertising or marketing. Hap never writes to Apple Health or Health Connect.

Workouts and weight are health information, which POPIA treats as special personal information. Hap reads them only with your consent, which you give by connecting (POPIA sections 11(1)(a) and 27(1)(a)). You can stop in Settings, and choose what Hap may read in Apple Health or Health Connect.

Hap's use of information received from Health Connect adheres to the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Camera, reminders and widgets

The camera is used while the scanner is open, to read barcodes, and when you choose to take a meal or recipe photo. Scanning saves and sends no photos or video. If you choose a photo from your library instead, Hap reads only that photo.

Reminders are scheduled on your phone. On an iPhone, the Today widget and the Live Activity read your diary on the phone.

Who else handles your information

Supabase Inc. hosts Hap's backup, sign-in and food catalog, under a contract that requires it to protect your information and use it only to provide its service to Hap.

Google (Gmail) delivers the sign-in emails, so it handles your email address and those messages.

Apple distributes Hap through the App Store, and Google through Google Play, each under its own privacy policy.

ntfy.sh delivers a notification to Hap's developer for each shared food or report to review. It receives the food's details, but never who shared or reported it.

Anthropic PBC, in the United States, runs the Claude AI that reads the meal and recipe photos you choose to send. It receives the photo but not your email or account, uses it only to answer the request, and under its commercial terms doesn't train its models on it. It may keep requests for a limited time to detect misuse.

On Android, the barcode scanner uses Google's ML Kit, which reads the barcode on the phone. ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage information, such as the phone model, the app version and an identifier for this installation of Hap, but never the camera image, and Google doesn't pass it on. On an iPhone, the scanner is Apple's and sends nothing.

Hap shares your information with no one else, unless the law requires it, for example under a court order.

Where it is stored

The backup is stored in Ireland, in the European Union. EU law (the GDPR) protects personal information at least as well as POPIA, as POPIA section 72 requires for a transfer abroad. Your consent when you sign in also covers this transfer.

Meal and recipe photos you choose to send are processed by Anthropic in the United States. Hap tells you this before you send one, and sending it is your consent to the transfer (POPIA section 72(1)(b)). Anthropic's terms also bind it to protect them.

How long it is kept

On your phone: until you delete it, or the app.

In the backup: until you delete your account. That removes your account and backup from the server straight away; copies in the hosting provider's routine backups are deleted automatically as those backups expire, within 30 days.

Technical logs: a few days.

Meal and recipe photos: not kept by Hap. The count of photos sent: two days.

Apple Health and Health Connect data: not kept by Hap. It is read again each time it's needed.

Foods you share: until Hap removes them. Your account ID is removed from them when you delete your account.

Keeping it safe

Your information travels encrypted (HTTPS), and the backup is encrypted where it is stored. The server lets each account read and change only its own information. Hap's developer reaches the database only to run and repair the service.

If a security breach affects your information, Hap will tell you and the Information Regulator as soon as reasonably possible, as POPIA section 22 requires.

Your rights

Under POPIA you can ask what personal information Hap holds about you, have it corrected or deleted, object to how it is processed, and withdraw your consent.

You can do most of this in the app: change or delete any entry, export your diary in Settings, Export, and delete your account in Settings, Account. For anything else, email hapapp.za@gmail.com. Hap replies within 30 days, and may ask you to confirm that the request comes from your account.

If you're unhappy with how Hap handles your information, please tell us first so we can try to put it right. You can also complain to the Information Regulator: POPIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za, 010 023 5200, inforegulator.org.za.

Children

Hap is meant for adults. Anyone under 18 may use it only with the consent of a parent or guardian, who must agree before a backup account is created. If Hap learns that it holds a child's information without that consent, it deletes it.

What Hap doesn't do

No advertising or tracking tools, no analytics of its own, and no profiles for marketing. No decisions about you are made automatically. Hap never writes to Apple Health or Health Connect, and it emails you only to sign you in, or about important changes to this policy or your account.

Changes

When this policy changes, the new version is published with a new date. If a change affects how your information is used, Hap tells account holders by email before it takes effect, and asks for consent again where the law requires it.
